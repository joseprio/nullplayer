NULLPLAYER

Privacy policy

Effective 2 October 2026

nullplayer collects nothing. There is no account, no analytics, no advertising and no crash reporting, and the app never sends your music, your library or anything about you to its developer or to anyone else. Everything it keeps stays on your device.

What the app stores, and where

  • Your music. Tracks you import are copied into the app's private storage and encrypted with AES-256. The key that unlocks them is protected by the Android Keystore and cannot be copied off the device.
  • Your library. Track details read from the files (such as title, artist, album and year), your groups and favourites, and a loudness measurement for each track are kept in a database in the app's private storage, which other apps cannot read.
  • Your settings. Kept in the app's private storage.

None of this is included in Android backups or transferred to a new device: the app opts out of both. Uninstalling the app, or clearing its storage, deletes all of it permanently.

What leaves your device

Nothing is sent to the developer. The app makes no connections to the internet. The only times information moves beyond the app are these, and each is something you choose:

  • Importing. When you share or open audio files in nullplayer, it reads them through Android. If you switch on deleting the originals, it asks Android to remove the files you imported once they are safely in the vault.
  • The upload page. Off by default. When you switch it on, the app serves a page on your local network so a computer on the same network can add, download or delete tracks. It is protected by a six-digit PIN shown on your phone, refuses further attempts after five wrong guesses, and stops when you switch it off. The page is served over plain HTTP within your own network, so use it only on a network you trust.
  • The notification and connected devices. While music plays, Android shows a media notification. Only if you turn on Name the track in the notification does it include the title, artist and album art, which Android then also shows on the lock screen and passes to devices that read the media session, such as a watch or a car.
  • Spoken announcements. When you ask the app to announce a track, the text is spoken by the text-to-speech engine installed on your device. Some engines may process speech online; that is governed by the engine provider's own policy.

Biometric unlock

The optional locks use Android's own fingerprint, face or screen-lock prompt. The app is told only whether you passed; it never receives or stores biometric data.

Permissions

  • Network access: only for the optional upload page on your local network.
  • Notifications and foreground service: to keep playing with the screen off and show playback controls.
  • Biometrics: for the optional locks.
  • Vibration: for the optional haptics that follow the music.
  • Audio settings and wake lock: to route sound to your chosen output and keep playback and uploads running.

Children

The app is not directed at children and, as above, collects no information from anyone.

Changes

If this policy changes, the new version will be published on this page with a new effective date. Its full history is in the repository.
